# Recipe Writing Rules

Rules for drafting and editing recipe files in the Obsidian vault. Shares language foundations with `blog-writing-rules.md` but optimized for instruction and brevity.

## Core Principle

A recipe is instruction, not storytelling. Write for the cook at the stove, not the reader at a desk. Every sentence should help someone cook better or understand a technique.

Backstory, anecdotes, and "this dish is like..." belong in the food log or an essay — not the recipe file.

## Structure

1. **Frontmatter** — required fields per vault schema
2. **Mechanic** (optional) — one paragraph on the ratio, technique, or principle that drives the dish
3. **Ingredients then Method** — grouped by component when the recipe has multiple parts (dal + tadka, dough + filling, etc.)
4. **To serve** — one line; what it goes with
5. **Variations** (optional) — only for meaningfully different branches in method or outcome
6. **Notes** (optional) — edge cases, substitutions, storage when non-obvious

Skip any section that has nothing useful to say.

## The Mechanic Section

Use it when:
- A ratio drives the outcome (lentils:water, butter:flour)
- A technique is non-obvious and changes the result (folding vs. mixing, dry-brining)
- A key substitution has real consequences

Skip it when the recipe is just "combine and cook."

One paragraph only. State the principle, state the consequence.

## Ingredients

- Format: `quantity, ingredient, prep note` — all on one line
- Be specific: "2 tbsp ghee" not "a knob of butter"
- Prep notes in plain English: "sliced thin," "rinsed until water runs clear"
- Group by component with a subheading when the recipe has 2+ distinct parts

## Method

- Imperative, present tense: "Add the lentils" not "You should add the lentils"
- One action per sentence
- Sensory cues first, time second: "until golden, about 3 minutes" not "cook 3 minutes"
- No passive voice: "Add the garlic" not "the garlic should be added"
- Include what to look for: colour, smell, texture, sound

## Variations

Include only when the variation is different in method or outcome, not just a swapped ingredient.

- Name the difference upfront: "thicker result," "adds smokiness"
- One short paragraph per variation
- Link the source if adapted from elsewhere

## Formatting

- **Bold** only for ratios and key techniques in the Mechanic section; nowhere else
- Em-dashes: avoid. Use commas, semicolons, or separate sentences instead
- Lists for ingredients; prose for method
- No sub-headers within a single component's method unless the component has 4+ distinct phases

## Language

### Spelling (Canadian English hybrid)
- British: colour, favour, behaviour, flavour, grey
- American: -ize endings (realize, organize), center, liter

### Units
Metric with optional imperial in parentheses: "250 ml (1 cup)," "180°C (350°F)"

### Quantities
- Spices measured in tsp/tbsp, not "a pinch" unless the amount is truly unmeasurable
- "A pinch" is acceptable for finishing salt, pepper, hing, and similar
- Temperature, weight, and volume are always specific

## Anti-Patterns

- No backstory or emotional framing in the recipe file ("A bowl of this is like a warm hug")
- No meta-commentary: don't introduce sections or summarize what you just did
- No hedge stacking: "you might want to consider possibly trying..."
- No vague verbs: "cook until done" → specify what done looks like
- No redundant signposting: "Taste and adjust" is enough; don't write "Taste and adjust seasoning to your preference"
- No corporate food writing: "elevate," "beautiful," "stunning," "perfectly balanced"
